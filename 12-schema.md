# 12 — Schema: the four statuses of a term

**What this is:** the classification scheme of the canon. Every term carries exactly one status, so a reader always knows whether a name is decided, proposed, unmeasured, or a look-alike pair that must not be confused. *(ES: el esquema del Study — cada término lleva exactamente un estado: decidido, propuesto, sin dato, o pareja que no se confunde.)*

## The four statuses

1. **RENAME-WINNER** — *(elegido por el rename)* the official English name, sealed under the Sovereign's sign-off. Its Spanish twin is a historical alias, absorbed — append-only, never rewritten in silence.
2. **RENAME-SUGGESTED** — *(sugerido por el rename)* a name coined once by the Desk in the ledger, not yet sealed, or not yet built in the project's own files. It waits for the sign-off or for its first real use.
3. **NO_DATA** — not yet measured in the project's files; no verdict is possible. Always with cause, never a guess. Distinct from RED.
4. **GREY AREA** — *(similares, zona gris)* two names that look alike but mean different things. The canon pairs them and forbids the confusion. Grey areas are dissolved by definition, not by counting.

## The lifecycle (migration law)

NO_DATA → measured → RENAME-SUGGESTED → (Sovereign's sign-off) → RENAME-WINNER. A winner never demotes; a wrong winner is corrected by a new ruling in the ledger, append-only. *(ES: nada retrocede; una corrección es una nueva dirimencia, nunca una reescritura.)*

## How to unify while learning

- Pick one GREY AREA pair, define both sides in your own words, then count each in the project — the counts show where the drift lives.
- Before coining anything, check the four statuses: coin once, reuse everywhere.
- A term without a command that shows it is a belief, not knowledge (the eye's rule).
- When a suggested term gets its first real use, propose its promotion: the sign-off is always the Sovereign's.
